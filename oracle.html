and astro dice

Cast the coins for the Book of Changes, in James Legge’s 1882 translation, cast the 24 runes of the Elder Futhark, or roll the three astrology dice. Hold your question in mind first.

Method

The three-coin method. Each line takes three coins, heads counting 3 and tails 2: 6 is a changing broken line, 7 an unbroken line, 8 a broken line, 9 a changing unbroken line, built from the bottom up. Changing lines turn into their opposites to give a second hexagram, read as where things are heading. The coins are your browser’s cryptographic random generator, with the same odds as real coins. The texts are Legge’s translation, The Yî King, Sacred Books of the East volume XVI (1882), public domain, in his own spelling; the short plain line under each name is ours.

Make a bind rune

A bind rune joins two or three runes on one stave, so their meanings work together, often as a personal sign or a focus for an intention. Tap up to three runes.

Method

Runes are drawn at random from all 24 without putting any back, each landing upright or reversed with even odds; nine runes look the same either way up and are always read upright. In the nine-rune cast each rune is also thrown onto a round cloth: it lands at a random point, about one in six rolls off the cloth and is set aside, and each lands face up or face down with even odds. Only face-up runes on the cloth are read, by how close they lie to the centre. The spread positions follow modern rune-reading practice; how runes were used for divination in the Viking age is barely recorded. The randomness is your browser’s cryptographic generator. Method

Three twelve-sided dice, as sold for astrological divination. The planet die (the ten planets and the Moon’s two nodes) says what is at work, the sign die how it unfolds, and the house die where in your life. Every face has the same odds, from your browser’s cryptographic random generator. Go deeper opens the full birth-chart texts for that planet in that sign and house.
