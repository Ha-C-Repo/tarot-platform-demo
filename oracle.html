I Ching
and runes

Cast the coins for the Book of Changes, in James Legge’s 1882 translation, or draw from the 24 runes of the Elder Futhark. Hold your question in mind first.

Method

The three-coin method. Each line takes three coins, heads counting 3 and tails 2: 6 is a changing broken line, 7 an unbroken line, 8 a broken line, 9 a changing unbroken line, built from the bottom up. Changing lines turn into their opposites to give a second hexagram, read as where things are heading. The coins are your browser’s cryptographic random generator, with the same odds as real coins. The texts are Legge’s translation, The Yî King, Sacred Books of the East volume XVI (1882), public domain, in his own spelling; the short plain line under each name is ours.

Method

Runes are drawn at random from all 24 without putting any back, each landing upright or reversed with even odds. Nine runes look the same either way up, so they are always read upright. Three runes read, in order, what has been, what is, and what may come. The rune shapes are drawn on the page; the readings are sample text written for this demo, in the common modern sense of each rune.
